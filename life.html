The Great Fool

Huang Gongwang, 1269–1354

Huang Gongwang was born Lu, in Changshu, and adopted as a boy into the Huang family — family legend says the elderly Huang who took him in exclaimed something like “I have waited ninety years for a son,” which is where the name Gongwang, roughly “hoped-for one,” comes from. He grew up clever and widely read, the kind of boy expected to pass the examinations and rise through the Yuan bureaucracy.

That career ended badly. Working as a minor clerk under a provincial official, Huang was swept up in his superior’s corruption case and imprisoned for a time in his forties — a decade that, by his own account, cost him the ambitions of his youth. When he was eventually freed, he did not go back to government service. He joined the Quanzhen school of Daoism instead, and spent years as a wandering priest and fortune-teller around the lakes and cities of the Jiangnan region, well outside the world he had been trained for.

A late start

He did not take up painting seriously until he was about fifty. Working mostly self-taught from the old masters — Dong Yuan and Ju Ran of the Southern Tang and Song, above all — he spent his remaining decades developing a style built from patience rather than display: landscapes made of hundreds of small, dry, textured brushstrokes laid down in layers, rather than a few dramatic gestures. It reads less like a young man’s bravado and more like an old man’s method — which is exactly what it was.

He is remembered today as the eldest and most influential of the Four Masters of the Yuan Dynasty, alongside Wu Zhen, Ni Zan, and Wang Meng — painters who worked outside the patronage of a Mongol court that had little use for the Chinese literati class, and who painted instead for their own cultivation and for each other.

His sobriquet, Dachi — “the Great Fool” — is the name a man takes when he has stopped needing the court’s opinion of him.

By tradition he died in his mid-eighties, still travelling the hills along the Fuchun River that he had spent his last decade painting.
